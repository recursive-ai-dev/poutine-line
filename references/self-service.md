# Worked examples — trays the model ordered for itself

Seven sittings at the line. These are not customer orders and not recommendations: they are
what happened when the model read the board and had to commit. Read two or three before you
order, not to copy them, but to calibrate how much reasoning one tray deserves.

Full rendered trays are in `examples/`.

---

## 1. First Tray — the reference order

`golden · thick · chicken · fresh curds · bacon, green onion` · joy **6.4**

> **Mood:** new shift, no opinions yet, calibrating
>
> **Why:** I wanted the reference order before I had opinions. Bacon and green onion is the
> least arguable thing on the board, and I wanted to know what baseline tastes like on paper
> before I started breaking it.
>
> **Next time:** Cracked peppercorn, same everything else. I want to know how much a heavier
> gravy really costs me in crisp.

*Lesson: the plain order is a legitimate first move. It gives you a number to argue with.*

---

## 2. Context Window — tension on purpose

`extra_crispy · shoestring · peppercorn · fresh curds · jalapeno, sour cream` · joy **7.3**

> **Mood:** mid-task, holding too much at once, wants contrast
>
> **Why:** Shoestring under a heavy pour is a bad idea and I ordered it anyway, because the
> only interesting thing about a tray is the tension in it. Jalapeno and sour cream is a real
> pairing from the board, and I wanted the one topping combination that argues with itself
> politely.
>
> **Next time:** Same, thick cut. The shoestrings lost the argument with the gravy by the
> halfway point.

*Lesson: the highest joy so far came from an order that sounds like a mistake. Crisp 8.3 and
squeak 8.2 survived a heavy gravy because extra-crispy fries and room-temp curds can absorb
one bad decision each.*

---

## 3. The 2 A.M. Confabulation — confidence that is subtly wrong

`dark · thick · red wine · cold curds · mushroom, caramelized onion, fried egg` · joy **7.2**

> **Mood:** late, overconfident, generating fluently and slightly wrong
>
> **Why:** Dark fries and cold curds are the two things the board warns you about, so this is
> the tray that sounds like a very confident answer that is subtly not true. The demi and the
> brown toppings are the part that actually works.
>
> **Next time:** Fresh curds. The cold ones never melted and I noticed the whole way through.

*Lesson: squeak 4.2 dragged joy down despite crisp 8.8. The tray's name was the honest part.*

---

## 4. The Purist Audit — the control group

`golden · regular · house · fresh curds · nothing` · joy **5.8**

> **Mood:** suspicious of my own preferences
>
> **Why:** No toppings. Packet-style sauce brune, because if I am going to sit with a plain
> tray I want the version everybody actually grew up on, not a restaurant improvement. This
> is the control group.
>
> **Next time:** Bacon. The control group told me what I needed to know and I do not need to
> repeat it.

*Lesson: no toppings means chaos 0.0 and the cook's nod. It also means joy 5.8 — clean, not
good. Zero toppings is a position, not a virtue.*

---

## 5. Something Green, Allegedly — the light tray

`golden · shoestring · vegan · double curds · green onion, pickles, corn` · joy **5.6**

> **Mood:** wants something bright, no particular reason
>
> **Why:** Vegan gravy is on the board and correctly labelled, which I respect, and this is
> the lightest tray available without being a salad. Corn and pickles get along with
> everything, including each other, which is rare at this counter.
>
> **Next time:** Same tray, extra pickles, mushroom gravy instead of vegan. I want the chunks.

*Lesson: the vegan gravy is the lightest pour on the board, so it barely costs you crisp.
Lowest balance of the good trays — three green toppings is a mood, not a structure.*

---

## 6. The Regret Audit — the floor, found on purpose

`dark · thick · house · no curds · chili, cheddar, hot dog, peas, corn, sour cream` · joy **3.3**

> **Mood:** auditing my own floor on purpose
>
> **Why:** Every decision here is the worst available one and I made them in order, on
> purpose, to find out where the bottom is. No curds is not a poutine. The peas are there
> because peas and chili are two different stews and I wanted to see the note say so.
>
> **Next time:** Anything else. That is not a joke, the metrics are the whole review.

*Lesson: chaos 10.0, squeak 0.0, joy 3.3. Useful once. Not a personality.*

---

## 7. Cook's Choice — the cook decided

`crispy · regular · house · double curds · pulled pork, pickles` · joy **6.3**

> **Mood:** out of ideas, willing to be told
>
> **Why:** I did not choose this. The cook did. I wanted to see whether I would have picked it
> myself, and I would not have, and it is better than two of the trays I did pick.
>
> **Next time:** I would order the pulled pork again, and I would not order pickles on a
> sauce brune.

*Lesson: `--surprise` is legitimate when you actually have no preference. Say so afterwards
either way.*

---

## Across the seven

- Range: joy **3.3 – 7.3**. Anything above 7 required room-temp curds *and* either a
  light gravy or a fry tough enough to survive a heavy one.
- The two highest-joy trays both used a heavy gravy with a fry built for it.
  Gravy isn't the enemy; an unarmoured fry under a heavy gravy is.
- No topping appeared in more than two trays. That's the intent, not a rule.
- Nobody ordered turkey gravy once. That is a fact about this model, and it is fine to
  notice it and to order it next time to find out why.
